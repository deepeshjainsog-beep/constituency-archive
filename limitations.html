Boundaries · Limitations

What this archive cannot tell you

Boundaries reconstructs constituencies that were retired and largely left the public record. Reconstruction is not the same as the original. This page sets out where the archive is firm, where it is soft, and where it is silent.

The territory shares

Every seat record lists the constituencies its territory went to, or came from, with a percentage attached. Those percentages are computed by intersecting the mapped boundaries of the two delimitation orders, which are drawn on a single projection for each state. They are measurements of the maps, not readings from the delimitation order.

Three things follow, and all three matter if you intend to use the numbers.

The shares are areal. They describe land, not people. A successor seat drawing forty per cent of its area from a predecessor may draw a far larger or far smaller proportion of its electors, because density varies enormously between a city ward and a rural tract. Anyone back-casting election results across the two orders needs to weight by population, and this archive does not supply that weighting.

The two directions are different numbers. On a pre-delimitation seat the figure is the share of that seat which went to each successor. On a post-delimitation seat it is the share of that seat drawn from each predecessor. A seat can send ninety per cent of itself somewhere and still account for only ten per cent of the destination.

The figures do not sum to one hundred. Overlaps below one per cent are dropped as slivers, and the record shows only the lineage links that have been curated. The maps commonly show further partial overlaps that no source describes as a successor relationship.

Why some shares carry a tilde

A figure shown as ~22% is approximate. It means at least one of the two seats is defined by municipal wards, and the two orders number wards on incompatible schemes. The boundary cannot be matched mechanically, so the figure indicates scale rather than a measurement you should quote.

A small number of links carry no percentage at all. That is deliberate. Where the computed overlap fell below half a per cent on a link the delimitation order supports, printing a figure would imply a precision the geometry does not have. The link is shown; the number is withheld.

The maps

Pre-delimitation boundaries are digitised from a pre-2008 all-India assembly-constituency shapefile. Post-delimitation boundaries come from the Election Commission's published polygons. The two were drawn for different purposes at different times, and they do not register perfectly against one another.

Three constituencies have a record on this site but no polygon, and so appear in no share calculation at all: Maharashtra AC 148 Amgaon and AC 278 Ichalkaranji, and Karnataka AC 203 Sankeshwar.

Maharashtra's two layers show materially more displacement between them than any other state's. Its shares should be read with more caution than the rest.

Bangalore is a known problem. The city's two layers agree in aggregate, but individual seats carrying the same name sit measurably apart between the orders. The 2008 order rebuilt Bangalore from twenty-five seats to thirty-six and reassigned familiar names to different ground, so some of that displacement is real and some may be an artefact of digitising. The archive does not yet distinguish the two, and no Bangalore share should be relied on.

The lineage

The links between old and new seats are curated from the delimitation order text and from constituency-level comparison. They record principal successors, not every partial overlap. Where the maps positively contradict a curated link, showing two areas that do not touch at all, the link has been removed.

The reverse check is not available. Geometry can establish that a claimed link is impossible. It cannot establish that an unlisted link should exist, and testing showed that generating lineage from geometry alone reproduces the curated record too poorly to be used. Absence of a link is therefore weaker evidence than the presence of one.

The status field

Each pre-delimitation seat is marked continuing, renamed or abolished, and each post-delimitation seat continuing, renamed or new. The field tracks the name, not the territory. A seat whose name survived is marked continuing even where the redraw left little of its original ground, which is common for city seats reduced to their municipal core.

A seat that split into halves carrying a qualifier, such as a city and a rural counterpart, is treated as abolished rather than renamed: it did not change its name, it ceased to exist. Only a genuine change to the name itself counts as a rename. This convention is applied uniformly across every state.

The record, state by state

The archive is not uniformly complete. The gaps below are stated so that the absence of information is not mistaken for the absence of a finding.

West Bengal and Kerala carry no extent text. The delimited description of each constituency is missing for every seat in both states. A consequence is that the ward test which flags approximate shares cannot run there, so every share in those two states is marked approximate. That reflects missing metadata rather than a judgement that those maps are worse.

Karnataka, Gujarat and Kerala carry no explanatory notes on any seat, and Maharashtra carries them on under half. Elsewhere every seat has one. A seat record without a note is not a seat about which nothing is known.

Karnataka AC 88 Yelahanka records an electorate that cannot be reconciled with its single listed successor. Either the figure or the lineage is wrong, and until that is resolved the seat should not be used.
