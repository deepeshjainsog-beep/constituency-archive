Submission Guidelines

What we are looking for from a contribution, and what we are not.

Write about power, not procedure

Delimitation redraws lines on a map. Those lines decide who wins elections. We want essays that show that mechanism at work: a leader who read a new boundary before anyone else did, a party machine rebuilt around a split seat, a caste or community bloc carved apart or stitched together on purpose.

Do not explain what delimitation is. Show what it did to someone.

Ground it in one place

Pick a seat. Pick a district. Pick a specific redraw and follow it all the way down. One constituency done properly beats a state-wide survey every time. Use our maps. Name the old seat, name the new one, show the exact territory that moved and who it moved for.

No slop

Every claim about a result, a margin, a leader needs a source we can check: the election, the year, the number. We will check it. Pieces that read like they were generated to hit a word count get rejected on sight, however polished the sentences look.

If you want to see the register we are after, read “Why India Should Redraw Its Electoral Map More Often, Not Less” on Swarajya. Tehsil-level numbers, named margins, one man followed through three elections, and a section at the end stating plainly what the evidence does not support. That is the standard.

Length

2,000 to 4,000 words. Longer if it earns it. We will break our own rule for an essay that is actually worth reading in full.

Pay

₹2,500 to ₹5,000. Based on how sharp the argument is and how hard you worked the evidence, not how many words you wrote.

How to pitch

200 to 300 words. The seat or district, the redraw you are looking at, and the argument you think it supports, with at least one concrete number or name already in hand. Send it before you write the full essay.

Send your pitch, or a finished essay if you have already written one, to
